About

A background across data, research, and software engineering

I am pursuing a Master of Science in Data Science at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities, with a minor in Computer Science. My graduate work has given me the opportunity to apply data science methods to healthcare problems involving both physiological signals and electronic health records.

At the Neuromodulation Center, I work with six-axis inertial measurement unit data to study gait and movement patterns in Parkinson's disease through time-domain and frequency-domain signal analysis. In my capstone research, I use the eICU Collaborative Research Database for unsupervised discovery and characterization of clinically meaningful ICU subtypes.

Before graduate school, I spent two years at Dell Technologies as a Software Engineer. My work there involved debugging, testing, validation, server environments, operating systems, and collaboration across engineering teams. That experience continues to influence how I approach data science: systematically, reproducibly, and with close attention to the details.

Research Experience

Graduate research in biomedical signals and critical care data.

My current research involves two different types of healthcare data: continuous IMU sensor signals and electronic health records from intensive care units.

01 May 2026 – Present

Volunteer Researcher — Parkinson's Disease Research

Neuromodulation Center

Department of Neurology · University of Minnesota
Principal Investigator: Prof. Biswaranjan Mohanty

  • Analyze and preprocess six-axis inertial measurement unit data comprising three-axis accelerometer and three-axis gyroscope signals from healthy participants and individuals with Parkinson's disease.
  • Develop a reproducible workflow for continuous, unlabeled IMU recordings to identify resting, walking, and turning segments, detect gait cycles, and classify healthy and Parkinsonian gait patterns.
  • Extract clinically relevant gait measures including stride length, step length, cadence, swing time, stance time, and walking speed.
  • Perform time-domain and frequency-domain analysis, including power spectral density and spectrogram analysis, together with feature extraction, cross-validation, and visualization.
Python Six-axis IMU Gait Analysis Welch PSD Spectrograms Parkinson's Disease
02 May 2026 – Present

Capstone Researcher — ICU Clinical Subtype Discovery

Division of Computational Health Sciences

Department of Surgery · University of Minnesota
Principal Investigator: Prof. Jocelyn Chi

  • Build a reproducible analytic dataset from the eICU Collaborative Research Database using first-24-hour demographic, laboratory, vital-sign, physiologic, and illness-severity variables for unsupervised clinical phenotype discovery.
  • Perform cohort selection, data-quality assessment, missing-value handling, repeated-measurement aggregation, feature engineering, and scaling before clustering analysis.
  • Evaluate K-Means and alternative clustering solutions using within-cluster sum of squares, silhouette score, cluster size, stability, consistency, clinical interpretability, and clinical plausibility.
  • Characterize ICU subtypes using clinical profiles, APACHE IVa severity, ICU and hospital mortality, and length of stay to assess clinical relevance.
